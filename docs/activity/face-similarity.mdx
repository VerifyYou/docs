Upload a photo you already hold of a person, such as the selfie or photo ID from your own KYC, and face similarity tells you whether it's the person who has been passing their HumanChecks. We compare your photo with the face from each check. That answers something a single check doesn't: whether the account is still with the person who opened it.

<Note>
Face similarity is in early access and is being turned on one workspace at a time. If a person's profile has no **Face similarity** card, it isn't on for your workspace yet.
</Note>

## Run an assessment

Anyone on your [team](/docs/workspace/team-and-roles) can run one.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the person">
    Open the person from [Activity](/docs/activity/reading-activity). The **Face similarity** card sits under the profile tabs. Select **Assess similarity**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add up to two photos">
    Add a photo of them, a photo of their ID, or one of each. Select **Add a photo** to pick a file, drag one onto it, or paste one with `⌘V` or `Ctrl+V`. Use a JPEG or PNG. Large photos are resized in your browser before they're sent, so the 5 MB limit rarely comes into play.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Run assessment">
    It usually takes a few seconds, and occasionally up to 20. The result opens in the same panel.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For each photo, we work out what it is: a photo of the person, a passport, a driver's license or another ID document. We check that it shows one clear face, then compare that face with the face from each HumanCheck this person completed with you.

## Read the result

The headline answers for the whole account.

| Headline | What it means |
|---|---|
| **The person in your photo** | Your photo matches the face behind this account's HumanChecks at 99% or more. |
| **Possibly the person in your photo** | A match between 80% and 99%. Similar, but not close enough to be sure. Lighting, angle or a changed appearance can do this, and a sharper, more recent photo usually settles it. |
| **Not the person in your photo** | One person completed every check on this account, and it isn't the person in your photo. |
| **A different person since** *date* | The checks on this account weren't all done by the same face. The list of checks shows which ones are the person in your photo. |
| **Couldn't read your photo** | We couldn't find one clear face to compare: either no face, or more than one. Try a sharp, front-facing photo of one person. |

With two photos that both read and agree, the headline says *your photos* instead of *your photo*. **A different person since** takes priority over the others, because when the face has changed, the real answer is in the list of checks. The percentage is how similar two faces are. It isn't the chance that they're the same person.

### Your references

Each photo you added gets a row with what we read it as (**Photo**, **Passport**, **Driver's license** or **ID document**), its file name, its match percentage and a verdict: **Match**, **Possible**, **No match** or **No read**.

With two photos, a line underneath says whether they show the same person as each other. If they don't, check you attached the right document. When your two photos agree on a match and at least one of them is an ID, we mark the result **Document-backed**.

We tell an ID from a selfie by the text printed on it. Document-backed means your two photos agree with each other and with the checks. It doesn't mean we checked the document is genuine, or who it names.

If none of your photos read as an ID, the result offers to add one. **Add photo** puts their ID in as a second photo. **Swap photo** replaces your second photo when neither read as an ID. Either way, the assessment runs again straight away.

### HumanChecks

This is every check we compared against, newest first, up to the 50 most recent. Each row has the date and time, the verification it ran under, whether it passed, and the match percentage and verdict for your photo. A check done by someone else's face shows **a different face**.

The list includes every check that proved this person's email or phone with you, whoever was in front of the camera that day. That is how a change of hands shows up. When you added two photos, the list scores the photo of the person, or the other photo if that one couldn't be read.

## Find an assessment again

Once someone has been assessed, the card on their profile shows the latest verdict, how many checks it matched (for example, `matched 3 of 3 HumanChecks`) and when it ran. **View assessment** reopens it, and **Assess again** starts a new one.

Every past assessment is on the person's **History** tab too, in a **Face similarity checks** card, newest first. Five show at a time, with a button to load the next five, and each opens as a read-only record. A record can't be re-scored, because the photos weren't kept. To compare again, run a new assessment.

## What we keep, and whose checks you see

We don't keep the photos you upload, as images or as face data. They're compared and discarded while the assessment runs.

The result is what stays: the verdicts, the percentages and what each photo read as. We also keep the file name you uploaded each photo under, so the history can tell you which photo was which. We save each assessment to the person's record, and that's what the **History** tab reads from.

You only ever compare against your own company's checks on that person. If they haven't completed a HumanCheck with you, the panel says **Nothing to compare yet**.

If a photo resembles someone else your company has verified, we note that on our side. It isn't shown in the dashboard.

Running an assessment doesn't change the person's [trust score](/docs/activity/guest-profiles) or the result of any check.
