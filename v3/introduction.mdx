---

VerifyYou confirms that the person on the other end of your app is a **real, live, unique human**, not a bot, a deepfake, or someone who already came through. You send them through a hosted verification flow; they come back with a result you confirm on your server.

## The whole flow in one picture

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a verification">
    Your server mints a session with [`POST /v3/initialize`](/v3/dev/spec/server-api/initialize) and a secret key, attaching whatever ties the run to your records. The browser opens it with [`vycheck({ session })`](/v3/dev/spec/browser-sdk/vycheck), as a full-page redirect, a drawer over your page, or an inline embed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="We verify the human">
    The hosted flow runs a short face check for liveness, then a uniqueness check against people who already verified. It works in place on phones and desktops alike, and a person on desktop can move the session to their phone with a QR code and be returned automatically. See [Humanness and uniqueness](/v3/concepts/humanness-and-uniqueness).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The result comes back to your app">
    Redirect mode returns the person to your page with `?vyt=<token>&vyc=<0|1>` on the URL. The embedded modes hand the same result to your `onComplete` callback with no redirect. Either way, read it with [`vyget()`](/v3/dev/spec/browser-sdk/vyget).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm on your backend">
    Exchange the token for the authoritative result with [`GET /v3/confirmations/{token}`](/v3/dev/spec/server-api/confirmations). Gate access on that, never on the URL alone.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Keys

Secret keys, in test and live flavors (`sk_test_…` / `sk_live_…`). Server only: they mint sessions and confirm tokens. Test keys see test runs, live keys see live runs, and a token from one partition reads as nonexistent under the other. Build with the test key, swap to live when you ship.

Publishable keys (`pk_*`) are deprecated. Start every session server side and open it in the browser with `vycheck({ session })`, which needs no key at all.

<Warning>
  Never put a secret key in client-side code. Confirm verification results on
  your server with the secret key. The `vyc` value the client sees is only a UI
  hint.
</Warning>
