You are building against a live API. This page tells you what we can change underneath you, what we cannot, and how you find out.

## The short version

- **`/v3` keeps its shape.** We add to it. We do not remove from it or narrow it.
- **Additive changes ship without notice.** New fields, new optional parameters, new endpoints, new values in a list.
- **Breaking changes get a new version.** `/v3` stays available while you are on it.
- **You hear about breaking changes by email**, at the contacts you give us, before they affect you.
- **Everything integrator-visible lands in the [changelog](/v3/changelog).**

## What we can change without telling you

We make these changes on an ongoing basis. Your integration must be resilient to them.

| Change | Example |
| --- | --- |
| A new field on a response | `band` appearing next to `score` on a confirmation |
| A new optional parameter on a request | `redirect_url` on `POST /v3/initialize` |
| A new endpoint | `POST /v3/keys/test` |
| A new value in an existing list | A new entry in `reasons` |
| A new error code for a case that already failed | A clearer `4xx` where you previously got a generic one |

Two habits cover almost all of it:

- **Ignore fields you do not recognize.** Do not reject a response because it grew.
- **Treat lists like `reasons` as open.** An unfamiliar value must not break your handler.

If your client rejects unknown fields, a routine additive release will look like an outage on your side.

## What counts as a breaking change

Anything that can stop a request you send today from working, or stop a response you read today from parsing:

- Removing an endpoint, a field, or a parameter
- Renaming any of them
- Changing the type or the format of an existing field
- Making an optional parameter required
- Removing a value we previously returned
- Changing what a value means while keeping its name

We treat the last one as breaking too. A field that keeps its name and its type, and starts meaning something else, is the hardest change to notice from the outside. We call it out in the changelog the same way we call out a removal.

## What our build enforces

We record the published shape of `/v3` in our source. Our build checks every change against that record. If a change removes an endpoint, a field, or a parameter, or makes an existing parameter required, the build fails and the change cannot merge.

Editing that record is how we break a version on purpose. Our process is that this only happens when we mint the next version.

This check runs on every pull request to the service that serves `/v3`. It is a floor. It does not replace the notice below.

## Versions and deprecation

A breaking change means a new version, served alongside the old one at a new path.

**We do not deprecate a version you are still on.** When `/v4` arrives, `/v3` keeps running. You move when you are ready, and you tell us once you have moved. We will ask, and we will tell you what changed and what the move takes. The date is yours.

We retire a version once every integration has moved off it.

## Pruning what you do not use

Between versions we prune parts of the surface that nobody uses in production. If your integration calls an endpoint, that endpoint stays. Where we are unsure, we leave it in place.

If you plan to start using something you have not called before, tell us. We will treat it as in use from that point.

## How you hear about it

**The [changelog](/v3/changelog)** carries every integrator-visible change: new capabilities, fixes you can observe, SDK releases, and changes of behavior. Check it when something looks different.

**For breaking changes we email you.** Give us the contacts who need to know. Most teams give us a mix of engineering, product, and whoever carries support. A change that affects people using your product needs to reach somebody who can act on it, and email does that.

Your dashboard shows the same information for your workspace. Treat email as the alert and the changelog as the reference.

**We know when you go live.** Once you run in production, we get an alert on our side. From that point we include your configuration in the compatibility checks we run before a release.

## What we ask of you

1. **Keep your contacts current.** Tell us when the right people change.
2. **Tell us when you go to production**, and when you move between versions.
3. **Confirm on your server.** Read the result with [`GET /v3/confirmations/{token}`](/v3/api/confirmations) using your secret key. The `vyc` value on the return URL is a UI hint, and the person can edit it.
4. **Pin your SDK version** and upgrade deliberately. The [changelog](/v3/changelog) tells you what each release changed.

<Note>
  Something changed and you cannot find it in the changelog? Tell us. If you
  noticed a change before we published it, that is a miss on our side, and we
  want to hear about it.
</Note>
