---
title: "Humanness and uniqueness"
description: "How we prove someone is a live human, then prove they're the only one."
---

Every verification answers two questions in order: **is this a live human?** and **is this human new here?** The first is the humanness check; the second is the uniqueness check. A person has to pass both.

## Humanness

Humanness is a short, guided **face check** using the device camera. The person centers their face in an oval and holds steady for about ten seconds. That is the whole thing: no documents, no license or passport uploads.

The capture is scored for confidence that a real, present person is in front of the camera, not a photo, a video replay, a mask, or a generated face. Below the threshold, the verification doesn't pass.

The capture itself doesn't stick around as a picture. It becomes a **face vector**, a numerical representation of the face's geometry, and the image itself is discarded. The vector is what the uniqueness check below runs on.

### Any device, including desktop

The flow runs wherever the person is:

- **On a phone**, the face check runs right there.
- **On a desktop with a webcam**, the flow runs in place too. No phone required.
- **On a desktop, continuing on a phone** is one click away: the page shows a QR code, the person scans it, and the same session continues in their phone's browser. The desktop follows along over a realtime channel, so the moment the phone finishes, the desktop returns to your site on its own. Nobody copies codes or refreshes.

## Uniqueness

Once we know the person is live, we check that they're new. The face code from the capture is searched against the faces of people who have already verified.

A match to **this same person's own record** is recognition, not a problem. That's how returning people get through quickly and how a person with a [bound identity](/v3/concepts/identity-and-recognition) is recognized on a new device.

A match to **someone else's record** is a **collision**: the same face already verified under a different person. Faces are compared inside your company, across every verification you run. What happens on a finding is a setting on the verification:

| Setting | Options | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Collision action** | `block` or `flag` | What a finding does. Block denies the verification outright. Flag lets it pass as `flagged` so you can review it, and the finding shows up in the confirmation's `reasons`. |

That comparison covers the whole company by default. A [`group`](/v3/dev/integrating/start-a-session#groups) narrows it, so you can reuse one verification across separate groups and each gets its own "one person, once".

One more face rule applies after sign-in: if someone signs in with their bound identity and the new capture doesn't match the face on their record, that's a **self mismatch** and it's reported the same way.

This combination is what lets you say "one real human, once": no duplicates, no second submission from the same face, scoped as tightly or as widely as your product needs.

## What you get back

A confirmation reports `verified: true` only when the person cleared **both** checks. The confirmation also carries a `status` (`approved` or `denied`; approved with findings is presented as flagged) and the `reasons` behind it, such as `collision_company`. You confirm that result on your backend; see [Confirmations](/v3/dev/spec/server-api/confirmations).
