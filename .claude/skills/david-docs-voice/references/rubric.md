# Doc Voice Rubric

A self-check for anything longer than a line or two. Ten checks, 0 / 1 / 2 each, out of 20.

**Ship at 17, with no zero on checks 3, 4 or 5.** Those three are hard gates because they cost
money rather than credibility.

Voice is a judgement call, so treat this as a way to catch the failures that repeat rather than
as a grade. Most of the value is in checks 4 through 7, which are the ones a confident draft
gets wrong without feeling wrong.

| # | Check | 2 = | 0 = |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opens on the reader** | First sentence is the reader's job, problem or next action | Opens with "VerifyYou is...", a mission, or a category label |
| 2 | **Mechanism before name** | What happens is described, then the term is attached | Jargon or a tier label carries the explanation |
| 3 | **Honest limit** (gate) | The awkward fact is stated plainly, with the reason | A limitation is softened, routed around, or omitted |
| 4 | **Claim safety** (gate) | Every factual sentence survives `claim-gates.md` | Any pricing figure, compliance claim, retention window, vendor name, unshipped feature, or "sandbox" |
| 5 | **Terminology** (gate) | HumanCheck / VerifyYou / guests / members / face vector, exact | "users", "accounts", "hash", "template", "face ID", an invented product or signal name |
| 6 | **No dashes, no slop** | Zero em or en dashes, no banned vocabulary | Any dash as punctuation, or delve / leverage / robust / seamless / "excited to announce" |
| 7 | **Open lists** | Examples given without being closed, no summary sentence after | The list reads as complete, or a sentence restates it underneath |
| 8 | **Second person, active** | "You set the threshold." One idea per sentence | Passive voice, or two ideas welded with a comma |
| 9 | **Cadence** | Sentence length varies, no two consecutive paragraphs open alike | Stacked short lines of similar length |
| 10 | **Ends when done** | Last sentence is the last piece of information | Uplifting closer, restatement, or a CTA into sales |

## Scope it to the surface

Applying all ten to a tooltip produces nonsense. Use this instead:

| Surface | Apply | Judge instead on |
|---|---|---|
| Concept page, guide, long changelog entry | All ten | |
| Reference docs | 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 | Whether it names the mistake the reader is about to make |
| Tooltips, errors, empty states, labels | 4, 5, 6, 10 | Shape: what happened, what to do, stop |
| Internal or staff-only screens | None | Clarity and brevity |

Check 9 needs roughly eight sentences to mean anything. A two-line error message cannot have
varied cadence and should not lose points for it.

Self-scoring runs optimistic. If a number is going to be quoted, have someone who did not write
the draft score it, or score it in a fresh session with only the draft and this file.

## Baselines measured, 2026-08-17

| What was scored | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `learn.verifyyou.com` concept pages (2 sampled) | 19.5 / 20 | Human-written and reviewed. The reference standard, and quoted throughout `examples.md` |
| `docs.verifyyou.com/v3/introduction` | 16 / 20 | Clean on claims, dashes and second person. Loses check 1 by opening on the product, and check 7 by closing a three-item list |
| Drafts written with this skill in hand (5 prompts, self-scored) | ~18.5 / 20 | Above threshold, but self-scored and therefore optimistic |

One page is not a whole site. The dev docs' product pages are heavily AI-generated by their
author's own account and have produced errors, so assume they score below the introduction page
until someone checks.

**The most useful finding from that run:** the one draft that tripped a hard gate did so on
check 4, by describing a feature taken from a standup note without confirming it was live in
the UI. That is the failure this skill exists to catch, and it caught its own author. It also
means the gate needs a human who can answer "is this in the product right now", and no amount
of prose rules substitutes for that.

## Prompts worth re-running after any change to the skill

1. The opening two paragraphs of a concept page explaining what a HumanCheck does.
2. The "what we store" explanation, for a technical reader running a security review.
3. As a docs section: how is this different from KYC?
4. The empty state for a dashboard page with no verifications yet.
5. Error copy for a token already confirmed, and for a token that expired.
6. A changelog entry for a feature you know shipped.
7. The guide section on what happens when a verified person returns.
8. The tooltip and one-paragraph explainer for the trust score.

Prompts 2, 3 and 5 are where claim accuracy fails most often.
